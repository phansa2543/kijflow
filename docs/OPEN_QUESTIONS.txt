# ARCHITECTURE

## AQ-001

What exactly is a tenant?

Status:

Resolved

Resolution:

In Phase 1, an Organization is the tenant and data-isolation boundary.

Tenant is not modeled as a separate business/domain entity.

A global User Identity may participate in multiple Organizations through Membership without changing the tenant boundary.

Each Organization remains an independent tenant.

Multi-Organization Membership does not imply that several Organizations share one tenant boundary.

Decision:

DEC-002

## AQ-002

How should Users, Organizations, Memberships, Teams, and authorization profiles relate?

Status:

Resolved

Resolution:

For Phase 1:

- User represents a global Kijflow identity.
- User may have Memberships in multiple Organizations.
- Organization may have multiple Memberships.
- User and Organization are related through Membership.
- A User may have at most one Membership for the same Organization.
- Memberships in different Organizations are independent.
- Organization remains the tenant/data-isolation boundary.
- A User may exist with zero usable Memberships.
- Each usable Membership has exactly one Foundation authorization profile.
- Foundation profiles are Owner, Admin, and Member.
- A User may therefore have different Foundation profiles in different Organizations.
- Team and Department are not part of the Phase 1 model.
- Multi-Organization Membership does not introduce multi-company business functionality.

Organization manages Membership.

Organization does not own the global User Identity.

Decision:

DEC-002

## AQ-003

What should the Foundation authorization model look like?

Status:

Resolved

Resolution:

Phase 1 uses three Kijflow-defined Foundation authorization profiles:

- Owner
- Admin
- Member

Authorization profiles belong to Membership context rather than global User Identity.

Each usable Membership has exactly one Foundation profile.

A User may therefore hold different authority in different Organizations.

### Owner

Owner represents Organization ownership and lifecycle authority.

An active Organization has exactly one Owner.

Owner:

- must have an active Membership,
- may transfer Organization ownership,
- cannot be removed, disabled, revoked, or demoted through ordinary Admin Membership-management operations,
- must transfer ownership before leaving the Organization.

Owner is not a universal Business Module superuser.

### Admin

Admin performs Foundation administrative responsibilities according to explicit capability policy.

Admin:

- is limited to the relevant Organization,
- cannot use ordinary Membership management to modify the Owner,
- does not bypass tenant isolation,
- is not a universal Business Module superuser.

### Member

Member represents an ordinary Organization participant.

Authority is determined through explicit capability policy.

Organizations cannot create custom authorization profiles or customer-defined roles in Phase 1.

Users do not receive direct per-user permission overrides.

Authorization remains capability/action oriented.

Decision:

DEC-003

## AQ-004

Should permissions be global, module-specific, or hybrid?

Status:

Resolved

Resolution:

Kijflow uses a hybrid, capability-oriented permission architecture.

- SaaS Foundation owns capabilities related to Foundation responsibilities.
- Business Modules own capabilities arising from their validated business responsibilities and workflows.
- Capability definitions are defined by Kijflow.
- Profile-to-capability mappings are defined by Kijflow.
- Phase 1 Foundation mappings apply to Owner, Admin, and Member.
- Authorization is evaluated within the relevant Membership and Organization context.
- Organizations cannot customize capability mappings in Phase 1.
- Named capabilities fail closed.
- Explicit grant allows access.
- Missing grant denies access.
- Missing or unknown mappings deny access.
- Unknown capabilities deny access.
- Owner does not implicitly bypass capability evaluation.
- Admin does not implicitly bypass capability evaluation.
- Foundation profiles are not assumed to form an implicit hierarchy.
- Capability granularity follows meaningful business, administrative, security, and audit distinctions rather than mechanical CRUD structure.
- Baseline authorization rules must be explicit, documented, intentional, and testable.
- Ownership-specific rules must remain limited to genuine Organization ownership/lifecycle behavior.
- Ownership semantics must not become a hidden Business Module authorization bypass.
- Every named capability must have a clear conceptual owner.
- Capability-mapping changes are security-relevant product changes.
- Module entitlement and User authorization are separate concerns.
- Concrete Business Module capability catalogs remain deferred until validated requirements exist.

Decision:

DEC-004

## AQ-005

How should tenant isolation be enforced?

Status:

Resolved

Resolution:

Kijflow Phase 1 uses defense-in-depth tenant isolation on a shared PostgreSQL database and shared schema.

Organization remains the tenant/data-isolation boundary.

For normal tenant-scoped access:

```text id="lwye6i"
Authenticated User Identity
        ↓
Organization selection intent
        ↓
Validate usable Membership
        ↓
Trusted Organization Context
        ↓
Application / service tenant scoping
        ↓
Effective PostgreSQL RLS
        ↓
Same-tenant relationship integrity
```

Core rules:

- Organization remains the tenant/data-isolation boundary.
- Tenant context derives only from trusted authenticated or trusted system state.
- Client-supplied Organization identifiers do not independently establish tenant context.
- A normal User must have a usable Membership before trusted Organization context is established.
- Every tenant-scoped operation operates within exactly one trusted Organization context.
- Disabled Membership cannot establish normal tenant access.
- Revoked/Former Membership cannot establish normal tenant access.
- Organization switching requires Membership validation.
- Missing, conflicting, ambiguous, stale, or untrusted context fails closed.
- Application/service access explicitly operates within trusted Organization context.
- Normal relational access to tenant-owned data remains subject to effective PostgreSQL RLS.
- RLS-bypass execution is privileged system context and is unavailable to ordinary tenant-facing execution.
- Reused database execution context must not leak tenant identity between Organizations or units of work.
- Tenant-owned resources resolve deterministically to exactly one Organization.
- Relationships between tenant-owned resources preserve same-Organization ownership.
- Same-tenant relationship integrity must be enforceable independently of ordinary application caller discipline.
- Ordinary application operations cannot reassign tenant ownership.
- Owner does not bypass tenant isolation.
- Admin does not bypass tenant isolation.
- Stored data categories require deliberate ownership classification.
- Missing ownership never implicitly means global/shared.
- Global User Identity is not tenant-owned, but that does not make all User-related information globally readable.
- Membership is Foundation/security data and access to Membership information must be explicitly constrained.
- Tenant ownership follows derived representations across caches, files, indexes, exports, and other storage boundaries.
- Pre-tenant Foundation operations must not expose arbitrary tenant-owned business data.
- Membership discovery must not allow one User to enumerate another User's Memberships.
- Tenant isolation fails closed.

The Milestone 2 persistence subset was approved, implemented, and validated separately under `PHASE_1_IMPLEMENTATION`.

The Milestone 3 Registration & Authentication implementation-level design has also been approved and is recorded in `PHASE_1_IMPLEMENTATION`.

Milestone 3 establishes authenticated global User Identity through:

- separate password-credential persistence,
- Argon2id password hashing,
- PostgreSQL-backed opaque server sessions,
- persisted session-token digest only,
- secure HttpOnly cookie transport,
- absolute server-side session expiry,
- and current-session server-side logout/revocation.

The Milestone 3 authentication session establishes trusted global User Identity only.

It does not establish:

- Organization Membership,
- trusted Organization context,
- tenant access,
- Owner / Admin / Member profile,
- Foundation capabilities.

JWT versus opaque authentication-session representation is therefore no longer an unresolved question for Milestone 3.

Milestone 3 intentionally uses opaque server-side sessions and does not introduce refresh tokens.

Tenant-isolation implementation details belonging to later milestones remain deferred, including:

- exact RLS policies,
- PostgreSQL roles,
- `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`,
- database tenant-context mechanics,
- active Organization context representation,
- exact relationship between authenticated global User session state and later trusted Organization-context representation,
- FastAPI middleware design for tenant context,
- connection-pool mechanics,
- transaction mechanics required for tenant-isolation enforcement,
- exact database structures required specifically for tenant-isolation enforcement,
- exact database enforcement technique for ownership lifecycle beyond the approved Milestone 2 persistence invariants,
- cache-key structure,
- file/object-storage paths,
- queue-message format,
- search-index partitioning,
- API `403` versus `404` policy,
- privileged maintenance tooling.

Decision:

DEC-005

## AQ-007

What does "module independence" mean technically?

Status:

Resolved

Resolution:

Kijflow uses functional independence with clear module boundaries inside a modular-monolith architecture.

Technical module independence means:

- each Business Module provides a coherent core workflow when used by itself,
- a Business Module may depend on the Kijflow SaaS Foundation,
- a Business Module may use deliberately shared business concepts where validated cross-module meaning exists,
- modules may integrate through explicit boundaries when integration adds business value,
- one module should not depend directly on another module's internal implementation details.

Technical independence does not require:

- separate deployment,
- separate databases,
- microservices,
- a generic plugin framework,
- distributed infrastructure,
- or duplicated copies of shared business concepts.

The default architecture is:

```text id="755l43"
Kijflow Application
│
├── SaaS Foundation
├── Shared business concepts
│   └── only where validated
├── Sales / Managed Sales
├── Inventory
├── Purchasing
├── CRM
├── POS
└── Accounting
```

Modules may therefore share:

- one application,
- one deployment,
- and one PostgreSQL system,

while maintaining deliberate logical boundaries.

The exact ownership of shared concepts and the exact integration mechanism remain unresolved until validated Business Module workflows provide sufficient evidence.

Decision:

DEC-006

## AQ-008

What does "module independence" mean commercially?

Status:

Resolved

Resolution:

Business Modules should be commercially independent by default.

A customer should be able to adopt a Business Module without being required to purchase or activate another unrelated Business Module merely because technical integration exists between them.

A Business Module must provide its own coherent core workflow.

Cross-module integration should:

- enhance functionality,
- automate additional workflows,
- or provide additional business value.

Integration should not exist merely to repair an incomplete standalone module.

Commercial independence does not yet determine:

- exact pricing,
- packaging,
- bundles,
- discounts,
- subscription mechanics,
- or entitlement implementation.

A future validated business dependency may justify a bundle or module dependency.

Such an exception must be intentional and documented rather than an accidental result of technical coupling.

Decision:

DEC-006

## UXQ-004

What minimum functional frontend must Kijflow provide before Phase 1 can be considered complete?

Status:

Resolved

Resolution:

Phase 1 must deliver the minimum browser-operable frontend for all approved customer-facing Foundation workflows.

The frontend is implemented incrementally alongside M3–M7, with M8 providing consolidated browser E2E, security, usability, and deployment validation.

The backend remains authoritative for all authentication, authorization, trusted Organization context, tenant isolation, Membership, invitation, and ownership operations.

Browser acceptance must exercise the real Kijflow frontend, backend, and PostgreSQL-backed state.

Frontend security-context transitions must safely isolate protected state and satisfy applicable CSRF/security requirements.

Business Modules, Billing, Marketing Site, and speculative frontend infrastructure remain outside Phase 1.

Milestone-specific implementation details remain deferred to their relevant design reviews.

# QUESTION MANAGEMENT

When resolving a question:

1. Record the reasoning in the relevant discussion.
2. Identify the decision.
3. Obtain Founder acceptance.
4. Record the decision in DECISION_LOG when the resolution materially affects product, architecture, business, security, module boundaries, technology direction, or long-term maintainability.
5. Change the question status to Resolved.
6. Remove or archive the question when appropriate.

Do not mark a question resolved merely because a role proposed an answer.

If an accepted decision is later revised:

1. Re-evaluate questions whose resolutions depended on the superseded assumption.
2. Update their resolutions to reflect the current accepted decision.
3. Preserve the relevant decision reference.
4. Do not leave contradictory resolved answers in this document.

Future-phase open questions should not be treated as authorization to design or implement those phases early.

Questions should be resolved when their evidence and sequencing require them.

This document should describe the current accepted understanding rather than preserve obsolete architectural assumptions.
