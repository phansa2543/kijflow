# PROJECT CONTEXT

## Business Management SaaS for SMEs

## 1. Project Overview

This project is a Business Management SaaS designed for SMEs.

The product aims to solve practical business-management pain points while remaining simple, understandable, and easy to use.

The project is being developed as a one-man software house.

The Founder is responsible for product direction, final decisions, and implementation.

---

## 2. Product Vision

Build a practical business-management SaaS for SMEs that provides useful business capabilities without unnecessary complexity.

The product should prioritize:

* simplicity
* usability
* practical business workflows
* maintainability
* modularity
* extensibility
* reasonable development complexity

The product is not intended to directly compete with large enterprise software companies.

Popular SaaS products in Thailand may be studied as references for:

* common business workflows
* customer expectations
* terminology
* feature patterns
* market conventions

However, competitor products must not automatically be treated as the correct design.

---

## 3. Product Philosophy

The product should solve real customer problems rather than maximize the number of features.

The team should continuously distinguish between:

* necessary functionality
* useful functionality
* optional functionality
* unnecessary complexity

Simplicity is a product requirement.

A feature should not be considered valuable merely because competitors have it.

---

## 4. Target Customer

Primary target:

Small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs).

The exact customer segments, industries, and ideal customer profile are not yet finalized.

These should be validated through product and market research.

---

## 5. Business Model

The intended business model is SaaS.

The exact:

* pricing
* packaging
* subscription structure
* module monetization
* customer acquisition strategy

are not yet finalized.

These decisions should be evaluated by the Product Manager and SaaS Strategy Consultant.

---

# 6. Development Strategy

The product will be developed incrementally.

The initial goal is not to build a complete business-management suite.

Instead:

1. establish a reliable SaaS foundation
2. build the first valuable business module
3. make that module usable in real business situations
4. validate the product with real customers
5. expand with additional modules over time

---

# 7. Phase 1 SaaS Foundation

Phase 1 will establish the minimum SaaS foundation required to support the first business module.

Approved scope:

- Customer / Organization context
- Authentication
- User management
- Minimal authorization / permissions
- Strong tenant / data isolation

Phase 1 explicitly does not include:

- billing / subscription / packaging
- advanced permission builders
- complex approval hierarchies
- enterprise SSO
- teams / department hierarchies without validated requirements
- multi-company enterprise capabilities
- custom workflow engines
- business modules

The foundation should prioritize:

- security
- correctness
- simplicity
- one-person maintainability

The technical architecture for Phase 1 is sufficiently established to proceed into implementation planning.

The tenant and Organization/User model has been established under DEC-002.

The authorization model has been established under DEC-003.

The permission architecture has been established under DEC-004.

The tenant-isolation enforcement architecture has been established under DEC-005.

---

# 8. Phase 2 — Sales Management

The first major business module will be Sales Management.

The goal is to build the module to a level where real customers can use it.

The product should reach a sufficiently usable and reliable state before the Founder begins selling it.

The exact scope of the Sales module is not yet finalized.

It must be derived from:

* customer problems
* real SME workflows
* business requirements
* usability considerations
* technical constraints

---

# 9. Future Modules

Additional business modules will be developed after the first module has reached sufficient maturity.

Modules should have:

* clear responsibilities
* clear boundaries
* limited unnecessary coupling
* understandable interfaces
* deliberate integration points

The exact future module roadmap has not yet been finalized.

---

# 10. Modular Product Philosophy

The intended direction is a modular SaaS platform.

Customers should eventually be able to use relevant business capabilities without being forced to adopt every possible module.

However, "module independence" is not yet technically defined.

It must be determined through architecture analysis.

Independence may refer to:

* business responsibility
* domain boundary
* deployment
* data ownership
* commercial packaging
* integration

These concepts must not be assumed to be identical.

---

# 11. Integration Philosophy

Modules should integrate when integration provides meaningful business value.

Integration should not exist merely because it is technically possible.

The architecture should minimize unnecessary coupling while allowing useful business workflows across modules.

The mechanism for integration has not yet been decided.

Potential approaches may include:

* direct application interfaces
* APIs
* events
* other appropriate mechanisms

The decision should be made based on actual requirements rather than architectural fashion.

---

# 12. One-Person Software House Constraint

The system is primarily built and maintained by one person.

Every significant technical and product decision must therefore consider:

* implementation effort
* maintenance effort
* operational complexity
* infrastructure cost
* learning cost
* debugging difficulty
* support burden
* cognitive complexity
* technical debt
* future scalability

Enterprise-level complexity should not be introduced without a concrete reason.

At the same time, short-term shortcuts that create severe long-term architectural problems should also be avoided.

The goal is a system that one person can understand, build, operate, and evolve.

---

# 13. Engineering Philosophy

The Founder wants to understand the system deeply and write the implementation code personally.

AI is primarily used for:

* analysis
* architecture
* education
* review
* challenge
* planning
* identifying risks
* exploring alternatives

AI should not replace the Founder's engineering work by default.

# 16. Important Unresolved Architectural Questions

The following broader questions remain intentionally unresolved and are not blockers for starting Phase 1 implementation:

What does "module independence" actually mean for this product?

What should each module own?

What should be shared across modules?

How should modules communicate?

How should future integrations work?

How much architectural flexibility is justified for a one-person software house?

These questions should be resolved only when concrete Business Module requirements provide sufficient evidence.
