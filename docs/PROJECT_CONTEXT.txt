# PROJECT CONTEXT

## Business Management SaaS for SMEs

## 1. Project Overview

This project is a Business Management SaaS designed for SMEs.

The product aims to solve practical business-management pain points while remaining simple, understandable, and easy to use.

The project is being developed as a one-man software house.

The Founder is responsible for product direction, final decisions, and implementation.

---

## 2. Product Vision

Build a practical business-management SaaS for SMEs that provides useful business capabilities without unnecessary complexity.

The product should prioritize:

- simplicity
- usability
- practical business workflows
- maintainability
- modularity
- extensibility
- reasonable development complexity

The product is not intended to directly compete with large enterprise software companies.

Popular SaaS products in Thailand may be studied as references for:

- common business workflows
- customer expectations
- terminology
- feature patterns
- market conventions

However, competitor products must not automatically be treated as the correct design.

Kijflow should use market and competitor research to understand:

- customer problems
- real business workflows
- common expectations
- avoidable complexity

The purpose is not to copy competitor requirements.

The guiding product principle is:

> We do not discover requirements merely to build what customers or competitors already have. We discover problems and workflows so Kijflow can decide what standard it should provide.

---

## 3. Product Philosophy

The product should solve real customer problems rather than maximize the number of features.

The team should continuously distinguish between:

- necessary functionality
- useful functionality
- optional functionality
- unnecessary complexity

Simplicity is a product requirement.

A feature should not be considered valuable merely because competitors have it.

Product decisions should favor:

- clear default behavior
- understandable workflows
- low setup complexity
- low support burden
- practical SME usability

Flexibility should be introduced only when validated requirements justify its cost.

---

## 4. Target Customer

Primary target:

Small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs).

The exact customer segments, industries, and ideal customer profile are not yet finalized.

These should be validated through product and market research.

The product should remain appropriate for Thai SME usage while avoiding unnecessary enterprise complexity.

---

## 5. Business Model

The intended primary product model is SaaS.

The exact:

- pricing
- packaging
- subscription structure
- module monetization
- customer acquisition strategy

are not yet finalized.

Pricing, packaging, Billing, and Subscription implementation are intentionally deferred until the first Business Module is sufficiently defined and usable.

The SaaS Foundation should not prematurely encode assumptions about future package or pricing structure.

Module entitlement, subscription state, and User authorization are related but separate concerns.

Phase 2 will establish the minimum Module Enablement Foundation required before Business Modules depend on Organization-level module availability.

Phase 5 will address Pricing / Packaging, Billing / Subscription MVP, production operation of the Kijflow Business Application, and commercial launch readiness.

The product will be developed incrementally.

The initial goal is not to build a complete business-management suite.

The accepted post-Phase-1 roadmap skeleton is:

```text

        ↓
Phase 2 — Module Enablement Foundation
        ↓
Phase 3 — First Business Module
          + Minimum Import / Export
        ↓
Phase 4 — Marketing & Growth Foundation
          + Public Kijflow Marketing Site
        ↓
Phase 5 — Commercialization & Launch
          + Production Kijflow Business Application
          + Pricing / Packaging
          + Billing / Subscription MVP
          + Commercial launch readiness
        ↓
        Paid Commercial Launch
        ↓

        ↓

```

This is a roadmap skeleton.

Detailed scope, milestones, acceptance criteria, technical design, provider choices, and implementation mechanisms for future phases should be decided only when the relevant phase approaches.

Phase 1 is not reopened or expanded by this roadmap.

The general development strategy is to:

1. establish a reliable SaaS Foundation,
2. establish minimum Organization-level Business Module enablement,
3. build the first valuable Business Module,
4. provide practical Import / Export where needed for adoption and portability,
5. establish a public Marketing & Growth surface,

7. launch for real paid customer use,
8. expand with additional Business Modules,
9. introduce real cross-module integration only when concrete module workflows justify it.

Architecture should be designed only far enough to support validated current requirements without blocking reasonable future evolution.

Implementation-level detail should be designed primarily for the current milestone or phase.

# 7. Phase 1 SaaS Foundation

Phase 1 establishes the minimum self-service SaaS Foundation required before the first Business Module.

The Foundation must support realistic SaaS identity, Organization, Membership, ownership, authorization, and tenant behavior without becoming a general-purpose enterprise platform.

## Core Product Model

### Global User Identity

User represents a global Kijflow identity.

A User is not owned by one Organization.

A User may participate in:

- zero Organizations
- one Organization
- multiple Organizations

through Membership.

### Organization

Organization represents the SME/business using Kijflow.

Organization is the tenant and data-isolation boundary.

Tenant is not modeled as a separate business/domain entity in Phase 1.

Each Organization remains independent from other Organizations.

Multi-Organization Membership does not imply multi-company enterprise functionality.

### Membership

Membership represents the relationship between a global User Identity and one Organization.

A User may have Memberships in multiple Organizations.

Memberships in different Organizations are independent.

A change in Organization A must not implicitly modify Membership in Organization B.

The same User may have different Foundation authority in different Organizations.

### Foundation Authorization Profiles

Phase 1 uses three Kijflow-defined Foundation profiles:

- Owner
- Admin
- Member

The profile belongs to Membership context.

An active Organization has exactly one Owner.

Owner represents Organization ownership and lifecycle authority.

Owner and Admin are not universal Business Module superusers.

Authorization remains capability-oriented.

### Trusted Organization Context

Every tenant-scoped operation operates within exactly one trusted Organization context.

Conceptually:

```text
Authenticated User Identity
        ↓
Organization selection intent
        ↓
Validate usable Membership
        ↓
Trusted Organization Context
        ↓
Tenant-scoped operation
```

Client-supplied Organization identifiers do not independently establish trusted tenant context.

### Membership Lifecycle

The Foundation must support the product semantics required for:

- Pending
- Active
- Disabled
- Revoked / Former

The exact technical representation of each state is determined by the relevant implementation milestone.

Milestone 2 establishes only the persistent Membership lifecycle representation required by that milestone:

- Active
- Disabled
- Revoked

Invitation and Pending representation remain deferred to the relevant later milestone.

Revoking Membership:

- removes access to that Organization,
- does not delete global User Identity,
- does not affect Membership in another Organization,
- must preserve historical attribution.

### Self-Service SaaS Behavior

The intended Foundation supports self-service User registration and Organization creation.

New User direction:

```text
Register User Identity
        ↓
Authenticate
        ↓
Create first Organization
        ↓
Create Membership
        ↓
Creator becomes Owner
```

Existing authenticated User may create another Organization and become Owner of that new Organization through a new Membership.

### Invitation Principle

Organization manages Membership.

Organization does not create or own another person's global credentials.

A person invited into an Organization should establish or use their own global Kijflow Identity.

## Phase 1 Scope

Phase 1 includes:

- global Kijflow User Identity
- Organization
- Membership
- self-service User registration
- self-service Organization creation
- authentication
- trusted Organization context
- minimum Membership lifecycle
- invitation-based Membership onboarding
- Owner / Admin / Member Foundation authorization
- Organization ownership protection and transfer
- minimal capability-oriented authorization
- strong tenant/data isolation
- automated security regression coverage
- minimum deployment readiness required before the first Business Module

## Phase 1 Non-Goals

Phase 1 explicitly does not include:

- billing / subscription / packaging implementation
- payment-provider integration
- Business Modules
- customer-defined roles
- direct per-user permission overrides
- advanced permission builders
- complex approval hierarchies
- enterprise SSO
- Teams / Departments without validated requirements
- parent/subsidiary hierarchy
- group-company hierarchy
- consolidated multi-company reporting
- shared inventory across Organizations
- shared business data across Organizations
- inter-company transactions
- custom workflow engines
- customer-offboarding export implementation
- data-retention purge implementation
- production-scale DevOps infrastructure

## Accepted Architecture

The accepted Phase 1 Foundation architecture is defined by:

- DEC-001 — Phase 1 SaaS Foundation Scope

# 10. Modular Product Philosophy

Kijflow follows the Business Module Independence Principle established by DEC-006.

Module independence means:

- functional independence,
- commercial independence by default,
- clear module boundaries,
- optional integration,
- and a modular-monolith technical architecture.

A customer should be able to use a selected Business Module for its coherent core workflow without being forced to purchase another unrelated Business Module.

Cross-module integration should add:

- useful automation,
- additional workflow capability,
- or additional business value.

Integration should not repair an incomplete standalone module.

Business Module independence does not require:

- separate deployment,
- separate databases,
- microservices,
- duplicated shared concepts,
- generic plugin architecture,
- or distributed infrastructure.

The default technical direction is a modular monolith.

Conceptually:

```text
Kijflow Application
│
├── SaaS Foundation
├── Shared business concepts
│   └── only where validated
├── Sales / Managed Sales
├── Inventory
├── Purchasing
├── CRM
├── POS
└── Accounting
```

Modules may therefore share:

- one application,
- one deployment model,
- and one PostgreSQL system,

while maintaining deliberate logical boundaries.

### Backend Architecture — DEC-011

Kijflow adopts Modular Monolith with Pragmatic Clean Architecture
as the default backend architecture.

The backend uses one FastAPI application, one PostgreSQL system,
and one deployment unit while maintaining clear module boundaries.

Each implemented module may separate responsibilities into:

- Presentation — HTTP endpoints, request/response schemas, and error mapping.
- Application — Use cases and workflow orchestration.
- Domain — Business rules and domain concepts.
- Infrastructure — Database persistence and external integrations.

Dependency principles:

- Domain must not depend on FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, or external services.
- Application must remain independent of HTTP presentation concerns.
- Infrastructure dependencies must have explicit boundaries where needed.
- Application bootstrap connects infrastructure to application workflows.
- Modules must not directly access another module's internal implementation.

Pragmatic constraints:

- Do not create interfaces for every function or class.
- Do not create empty layers or speculative abstractions.
- Do not introduce Microservices or distributed infrastructure by default.
- Maintainability for a one-person software house takes priority over architectural purity.

M3 Authentication will be refactored incrementally while preserving
existing behavior, automated tests, and approved security requirements.

Exact folder placement and dependency contracts remain subject to
implementation-level Tech Lead and QA review.

DEC-011 extends DEC-006 without changing the approved Phase 1 scope
or milestone boundaries.

Shared business concepts must not be created speculatively.

Concepts such as Customer or Product/Service may later prove to be shared, but their ownership must be derived from validated business workflows rather than assumed in advance.

Commercial independence establishes the default product principle.

It does not yet determine:

- pricing,
- packaging,
- bundles,
- discounts,
- subscription mechanics,
- or exact entitlement implementation.

# 11. Integration Philosophy

Modules should integrate when integration provides meaningful business value.

Cross-module integration is optional by default.

Integration should:

- enhance a workflow,
- automate useful cross-module behavior,
- or provide additional value.

Integration should not exist merely because it is technically possible.

One Business Module should not depend directly on another module's internal implementation details.

Cross-module behavior should pass through deliberate boundaries.

The exact communication and integration mechanisms remain unresolved until concrete workflow requirements exist.

Potential mechanisms may include:

- direct application/service interfaces,
- internal APIs,
- domain/application events,
- or another sufficiently simple mechanism.

Kijflow should prefer the simplest mechanism that preserves the required boundary.

Distributed infrastructure such as:

- message brokers,
- generic event buses,
- plugin frameworks,
- service meshes,
- or microservices

should not be introduced merely to satisfy an abstract idea of modularity.

The appropriate integration mechanism should follow actual workflow, ownership, consistency, and failure-handling requirements.

DEC-007 intentionally places the first real cross-module integration after the second Business Module so that integration behavior can be derived from concrete participating workflows rather than speculation.

# 12. One-Person Software House Constraint

The system is primarily built and maintained by one person.

Every significant technical and product decision must therefore consider:

- implementation effort
- maintenance effort
- operational complexity
- infrastructure cost
- learning cost
- debugging difficulty
- support burden
- cognitive complexity
- technical debt
- future scalability

Enterprise-level complexity should not be introduced without concrete justification.

At the same time, short-term shortcuts that create severe long-term architecture or security problems should be avoided.

The goal is a system that one person can:

- understand
- build
- debug
- test
- operate
- support
- evolve

Reasonable extensibility is desirable.

Speculative flexibility is not.

This constraint applies not only to application architecture but also to:

- Marketing & Growth operations,
- SEO/content production,
- production infrastructure,
- Billing / Subscription,
- customer onboarding,
- support,
- Import / Export,
- and future module integration.
